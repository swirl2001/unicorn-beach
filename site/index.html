What's a unicorn day?

Three or more daylight hours in a row with:

≤ 1 ftwaves just off China Beach
≤ 5.6 mphwind
≤ 9 mphgusts

From 2021 to 2025 this happened about twice a year, mostly October to January, usually in the morning when a small, long-period swell meets a calm high-pressure day.

Notes on the data

What counts as a unicorn day

  • At least 3 daylight hours in a row with waves of 1 ft or less just off China Beach, wind of 5.6 mph or less and gusts of 8.9 mph or less.
  • From 2021 to 2025 that happened about twice a year, mostly on October–January mornings.

Where the history comes from

  • Waves: CDIP's wave model for the spot just off China Beach (MOP SF054, about 10 m deep), hourly, 2021–2025. CDIP is Scripps Institution of Oceanography's wave program.
  • Wind: the Fort Point weather station at the Golden Gate (NOAA NDBC FTPC1).
  • The limits above were chosen so that only the rare, truly glassy days qualify.

The live forecast

  • Runs automatically at about 5:17 am and 5:17 pm Pacific on GitHub Actions.
  • Waves, days 1–3: CDIP's wave forecast for the grid cell off China Beach. Days 4–7: Open-Meteo's offshore wave forecast × 0.37, the typical ratio of China Beach waves to the SF Bar buoy in 2021–2025.
  • Wind, gusts and rain chance: National Weather Service hourly forecast for the beach. Active NWS marine warnings (small craft, high surf and the like) cut the chance to a fifth.
  • Daylight only: hours count once the sun is 3° above the horizon.

How the chance is worked out

  • For each daylight hour: the chance that waves, wind and gusts all stay under the limits, allowing for forecast error that grows the further ahead it looks, times the chance it doesn't rain.
  • Each day shows its best 3-hour window, with that window's waves, wind, gusts and rain.
  • The short daily summary is written from those numbers: tiny waves (1 ft or less), small (up to 2.5 ft), big surf (up to 5 ft) or huge; calm wind (within the unicorn limits), breezy (up to 12 mph) or windy; plus rain likely (50%+) and surf advisory.
  • No archive of past forecasts was available to check these chances against real outcomes, so treat them as a careful estimate, not a tested probability.

Tides

  • NOAA tide predictions for Fort Point (station 9414290), sunrise to midnight, in feet above mean lower low water. Range is the day's highest tide minus its lowest.

Alerts

  • An email goes out once per date when a day within 48 hours reaches a 50% chance.
